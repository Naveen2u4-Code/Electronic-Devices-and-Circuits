TOPIC 2.9

Breakdown Mechanisms: Avalanche vs Zener

Why the reverse current suddenly soars, and the two different ways it happens

What breakdown is

In reverse bias the current stays tiny and almost constant (topic 2.4). But the electric field in the depletion region keeps growing with the voltage. For an abrupt junction the field is triangular, with its peak at the junction:

Emax = 2(Vbi + VR) / W

When Emax reaches a CRITICAL FIELD of a few hundred kilovolts per centimetre, the reverse current suddenly increases by many orders of magnitude while the voltage hardly changes. This is REVERSE BREAKDOWN, at the BREAKDOWN VOLTAGE VBR. For the example junction (the lighter n side, ND = 1016 cm−3, sets it), VBR ≈ 60 V. The field is 1.3 × 105 V/cm at 5 V and about 4.1 × 105 V/cm at 60 V, where the depletion region is 2.9 µm wide.

Breakdown is NOT DESTRUCTIVE in itself. Past the knee the current must be limited by the circuit (here a 1 kΩ resistor); the diode is only damaged if the power VBR·I heats it too much. Diodes designed to work in breakdown are called ZENER DIODES (or breakdown diodes) and are used as voltage references (topics 3.1 and 3.2).

Key points

  • Emax = 2(Vbi + VR)/W grows with reverse voltage.
  • At a critical field the current soars at almost constant voltage VBR.
  • Example junction: VBR ≈ 60 V, Emax ≈ 4.1 × 105 V/cm.
  • Not destructive if the circuit limits the current and power.

Avalanche breakdown: impact ionization

The few carriers that make up the reverse current are accelerated by the field across the depletion region. Between collisions with the lattice they gain kinetic energy. When the field is strong enough, an electron gains more than about Eg before a collision and can knock a valence electron free: IMPACT IONIZATION, which creates a new electron-hole pair. The new carriers are accelerated too and create more pairs: a chain reaction, the AVALANCHE.

The growth is described by a MULTIPLICATION FACTOR M, the number of carriers leaving the depletion region for each one entering it. A widely used empirical formula is

M = 1 / [1 − (VR/VBR)n], n ≈ 3 to 6

With n = 4: M ≈ 1.07 at half of VBR, 2.9 at 90%, and 25 at 99%. M grows without limit as VR → VBR, which is why the knee is so sharp. Avalanche needs a wide depletion region so the carriers have room to gain energy and multiply: it is the mechanism in LIGHTLY DOPED junctions with VBR above about 6–7 V.

Key points

  • Carriers gain energy in the field and create new pairs by impact ionization.
  • Chain reaction: M = 1/[1 − (VR/VBR)n], unlimited at VBR.
  • Needs a wide depletion region: lightly doped junctions, VBR above about 6–7 V.

Zener breakdown: tunnelling

In a junction with BOTH SIDES HEAVILY DOPED the depletion region is extremely thin, a few tens of nanometres, and the field is enormous even at a few volts. On the band diagram, the bands are so steep that the filled valence band on the p side lies opposite the empty conduction band on the n side, separated by only a thin barrier. Its width is roughly

d ≈ Eg / (qE)

When d shrinks to about 10 nm, electrons can TUNNEL straight through the forbidden gap, from the p-side valence band into the n-side conduction band, without needing any energy: a quantum-mechanical effect. No collisions, no multiplication: the field pulls electrons directly out of their bonds. This is ZENER BREAKDOWN, named after Clarence Zener, who explained it in 1934.

For a junction with NA = ND = 2 × 1018 cm−3, W is only 35 nm at zero bias. At 2.3 V the peak field reaches about 106 V/cm, and the tunnel distance is about 12 nm. Because tunnelling grows gradually as d shrinks, the Zener knee is SOFTER than the avalanche knee.

Key points

  • Heavy doping on both sides: W of tens of nm, field near 106 V/cm.
  • Electrons tunnel from the p valence band to the n conduction band through a gap d ≈ Eg/qE.
  • No multiplication; the knee is softer. VBR below about 4–5 V.

Doping decides the mechanism

Both mechanisms need a strong field, but avalanche also needs room. Heavier doping makes the depletion region thinner and the breakdown voltage lower. For a one-sided abrupt silicon junction, Sze's empirical fit gives

VBR ≈ 60 V · (Eg/1.1 eV)3/2 · (NB/1016 cm−3)−3/4

where NB is the doping of the lighter side: about 340 V at 1015, 60 V at 1016, 11 V at 1017 and 1.9 V at 1018 cm−3. Over the same range the width at breakdown falls from 21 µm to 0.06 µm, and the critical field rises from 3.2 × 105 to 9.5 × 105 V/cm.

A useful rule (Sze): breakdown below about 4Eg/q ≈ 4.5 V is by tunnelling, above about 6Eg/q ≈ 6.7 V by avalanche, and in between both contribute. All "Zener diodes" are sold under the same name, but above about 6–7 V they actually work by avalanche.

Key points

  • Heavier doping → thinner W → lower VBR (∝ NB−3/4).
  • Below about 4.5 V: Zener (tunnelling). Above about 6.7 V: avalanche. Between: both.
  • Example junction (1016 cm−3): 60 V, avalanche.

Telling them apart: temperature

The two mechanisms respond to temperature in OPPOSITE ways, which is the classic way to tell them apart:

  • AVALANCHE: POSITIVE TEMPERATURE COEFFICIENT. A hotter lattice vibrates more, so carriers collide more often and gain less energy between collisions. A higher field, so a higher voltage, is needed to ionize: VBR rises with temperature.
  • ZENER: NEGATIVE TEMPERATURE COEFFICIENT. The band gap shrinks as temperature rises (topic 2.6), so the barrier d ≈ Eg/qE gets thinner and tunnelling happens at a lower voltage: VZ falls with temperature.

Typical values for commercial Zener diodes are around −0.05% per °C for a 3.3 V diode and +0.08% per °C for a 12 V diode (the figures below use these as illustrations). Diodes of about 5–6 V, where both mechanisms act, have a temperature coefficient close to ZERO, which makes them the preferred choice for stable references. The avalanche knee is also sharper than the Zener knee.

Key points

  • Avalanche: VBR rises with T (more collisions). Positive TC.
  • Zener: VZ falls with T (Eg shrinks). Negative TC.
  • About 5–6 V: the two cancel, TC ≈ 0.

Summary

Reverse breakdown happens when the peak field in the depletion region reaches a critical value; the current then rises steeply at almost constant voltage. In lightly doped junctions the depletion region is wide and carriers multiply by impact ionization (avalanche): high VBR, sharp knee, positive temperature coefficient. In heavily doped junctions the depletion region is only tens of nanometres wide and electrons tunnel across the gap (Zener): VBR below about 4.5 V, softer knee, negative temperature coefficient. Between about 4.5 and 6.7 V both act, and near 5–6 V the temperature coefficient is close to zero.

Key points

  • Breakdown: critical field reached; current soars at nearly constant voltage. Not destructive if limited.
  • Avalanche: impact ionization, light doping, VBR > 6.7 V, sharp knee, TC > 0.
  • Zener: tunnelling, heavy doping, VBR < 4.5 V, soft knee, TC < 0.
  • 5–6 V diodes: both mechanisms, TC ≈ 0.

A metal–semiconductor junction: low forward drop and almost no switching delay

Recap

Key points

  • A Schottky diode is a metal–semiconductor junction (metal on lightly doped n-type Si). The metal is the anode. Symbol: a diode with S-shaped hooks on the cathode bar.
  • On contact, electrons flow from the semiconductor into the metal (φm > φs), leaving a depletion region and a barrier: φB = φm − χ from the metal side and V0 from the semiconductor side.
  • Forward bias lowers the semiconductor-side barrier and electrons flow into the metal. Conduction is by majority carriers only, so there is no stored minority charge and practically no reverse recovery time: it switches in picoseconds.
  • Forward drop is only about 0.2–0.4 V (vs 0.6–0.7 V for Si pn), so less power is lost (P = VFI).
  • Drawbacks: higher reverse leakage (rises with temperature) and lower breakdown voltage (typically 20–100 V).
  • Uses: rectifiers in switch-mode power supplies, freewheeling and reverse-polarity protection, solar-panel bypass diodes (3.5), RF mixers and detectors, and clamping transistors in Schottky TTL logic. A heavily doped metal–semiconductor contact is ohmic instead of rectifying.

  • Electronic Devices and Circuits, David A. Bell. Section on Schottky diodes.

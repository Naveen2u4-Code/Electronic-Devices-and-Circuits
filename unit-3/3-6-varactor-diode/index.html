Recap

Key points

  • A reverse-biased junction is a capacitor: the neutral p and n regions are the plates, the carrier-free depletion region is the dielectric. C = εA/W.
  • Raising the reverse voltage widens W, so C falls: a voltage-variable capacitor. A diode made for this is a varactor (varicap, tuning diode), symbol = diode with capacitor plates. It is always reverse biased.
  • C = C0/(1 + VR/V0)n: n = ½ for an abrupt junction (C ∝ 1/√VR), ⅓ for linearly graded, about 1 to 2 for hyperabrupt.
  • The capacitance ratio Cmax/Cmin sets the tuning range; hyperabrupt diodes give the largest ratio.
  • With an inductor it forms a tank of resonant frequency f = 1/(2π√(LC)); raising VT lowers C and raises f. A large series resistor isolates the bias supply, and a blocking capacitor stops the coil shorting the DC.
  • Uses: electronic tuning of radio and TV receivers, voltage-controlled oscillators (VCOs) in PLLs and frequency synthesisers, FM modulators, automatic frequency control and tunable filters.

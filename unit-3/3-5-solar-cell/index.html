Recap

Key points

  • A solar cell is a large-area pn junction (thin n⁺ emitter on a thick p base) with metal fingers on top, an anti-reflection coating and a back contact. It needs no bias.
  • Absorbed photons create electron-hole pairs; the built-in field sends electrons to the n side and holes to the p side. Open circuit gives Voc ≈ 0.6 V (Si); a load draws current. The p side is the + terminal.
  • It is a photodiode in the photovoltaic mode (4th quadrant). Output: I = Isc − I0(eV/VT − 1).
  • Power P = VI is zero at both short and open circuit and peaks at the maximum power point (Vm, Im).
  • Fill factor FF = VmIm/(VocIsc) ≈ 0.8. Efficiency η = Pm/Pin, with full sun = 1000 W/m². Silicon cells reach about 20 to 25 %; the single-junction limit is about 33 %.
  • Isc ∝ sunlight; Voc = VT ln(Isc/I0 + 1) changes only logarithmically. Heat lowers Voc by about 2 mV/°C and power by about 0.4 %/°C.
  • Series cells add voltage, parallel strings add current. Shading one series cell throttles the string; bypass diodes limit the loss.
  • Uses: rooftop and utility solar power, satellites, calculators, solar chargers, remote and off-grid equipment.

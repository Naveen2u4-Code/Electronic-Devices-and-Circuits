TOPIC 5.7

Regions of Operation: Cutoff, Active, Saturation

Two junctions, two bias choices each: four ways a BJT can operate

Four possible modes

A BJT has two junctions, and each one can be either forward biased or reverse biased. That gives four possible combinations, called the modes or regions of operation:

  • CUTOFF: emitter-base junction reverse (or not enough forward bias), collector-base junction reverse. The transistor is OFF.
  • ACTIVE (forward active): emitter-base forward, collector-base reverse. The transistor AMPLIFIES: IC = β·IB.
  • SATURATION: emitter-base forward, collector-base forward. The transistor is fully ON.
  • REVERSE ACTIVE: emitter-base reverse, collector-base forward. The emitter and collector swap roles; it works poorly and is rarely used.

Amplifiers use the active region. Switches use cutoff (OFF) and saturation (ON).

Key points

  • Two junctions, each forward or reverse biased: four modes.
  • Cutoff (both reverse) is OFF; saturation (both forward) is fully ON.
  • Active (EBJ forward, CBJ reverse) amplifies: IC = β·IB.
  • Reverse active swaps emitter and collector and is rarely used.

Cutoff region

When the base-emitter voltage is below the cut-in voltage (about 0.5 V for silicon), the emitter injects almost no carriers into the base. The base current is essentially zero, and so is the collector current, apart from a tiny leakage current ICEO.

In the example circuit (VCC = 10 V, RC = 1 kΩ), almost no current flows through RC, so there is almost no voltage drop across it. The collector voltage is therefore almost equal to the supply: VCE ≈ VCC = 10 V.

The transistor behaves like an OPEN SWITCH between collector and emitter.

Key points

  • VBE below the cut-in voltage (about 0.5 V): the emitter injects almost nothing.
  • IB ≈ 0 and IC ≈ 0, apart from the tiny leakage ICEO.
  • No drop across RC, so VCE ≈ VCC = 10 V.
  • Collector to emitter acts as an open switch.

Active region

When VBE reaches about 0.7 V, base current flows and the transistor enters the active region, as long as the collector-base junction stays reverse biased (VCE greater than about 0.3 V).

In this region the collector current is controlled by the base current: IC = β·IB. The collector voltage is VCE = VCC − IC·RC, which lies somewhere between the supply voltage and a few tenths of a volt.

Example (β = 100): VIN = 1.8 V gives IB = (1.8 − 0.7) / 22 kΩ = 50 µA, so IC = 5 mA and VCE = 10 − 5 mA × 1 kΩ = 5 V.

The active region is used for AMPLIFICATION: a small change in base current produces a proportional, β times larger change in collector current.

Key points

  • EBJ forward (VBE ≈ 0.7 V), CBJ reverse (VCE above about 0.3 V).
  • IC = β·IB and VCE = VCC − IC·RC.
  • Example: VIN = 1.8 V gives IB = 50 µA, IC = 5 mA, VCE = 5 V.
  • Used for amplification: ΔIC is β times ΔIB.

Saturation region

If the base current keeps increasing, IC = β·IB would keep increasing too, but the circuit cannot supply unlimited current. The largest possible collector current is set by the supply and the collector resistor:

IC(sat) = (VCC − VCE(sat)) / RC, with VCE(sat) ≈ 0.2 V.

In the example, IC(sat) = (10 − 0.2) / 1 kΩ ≈ 9.8 mA. The transistor saturates when β·IB exceeds this, that is when IB > 98 µA (VIN above about 2.9 V).

In saturation the collector voltage has fallen below the base voltage, so the collector-base junction also becomes forward biased. Both junctions are forward biased, VCE is only about 0.2 V, and IC no longer increases with IB: IC < β·IB. The transistor behaves like a CLOSED SWITCH.

Extra charge is stored in the base during saturation. It must be removed before the transistor can turn off, which slows switching (topic 5.8).

Key points

  • IC(sat) = (VCC − VCE(sat))/RC ≈ 9.8 mA in the example.
  • Saturation starts when β·IB exceeds IC(sat): IB > 98 µA, VIN above about 2.9 V.
  • Both junctions forward, VCE ≈ 0.2 V, IC < β·IB: a closed switch.
  • Stored base charge must be removed before turn-off, which slows switching.

Reverse active region

If the emitter-base junction is reverse biased and the collector-base junction forward biased, the collector acts as the emitter and the emitter as the collector. Because the collector is not designed to inject carriers efficiently (it is lighter doped and larger), the current gain in this mode is very small, often around 1 or less. This mode is rarely used in normal circuits.

Key points

  • EBJ reverse, CBJ forward: collector and emitter swap roles.
  • The collector is lighter doped and larger, so it injects poorly.
  • Current gain is very small, often around 1 or less.
  • Rarely used in normal circuits.

The load line: seeing all regions at once

For the circuit, VCE and IC are always related by the collector loop: VCE = VCC − IC·RC.

Plotted on the output characteristics, this is a straight line called the DC LOAD LINE. It meets the VCE axis at VCC (IC = 0, cutoff end) and the IC axis at VCC/RC (VCE = 0, saturation end). In the example, the load line runs from 10 V on the VCE axis to 10 mA on the IC axis.

The actual operating point, called the Q-POINT (quiescent point), is where the load line crosses the output curve for the present base current. As the input increases from zero, the Q-point slides up the load line: it starts at the bottom in cutoff, moves through the active region, and ends at the top in saturation.

Key points

  • DC load line: VCE = VCC − IC·RC, from VCC on the VCE axis to VCC/RC on the IC axis.
  • The Q-point is where the load line meets the curve for the present IB.
  • Raising VIN slides Q up the line: cutoff, then active, then saturation.
  • Changing RC or VCC moves the load line and the region boundaries.

Summary

Cutoff: both junctions reverse, IC ≈ 0, VCE ≈ VCC, OFF switch. Active: EBJ forward, CBJ reverse, IC = β·IB, used for amplification. Saturation: both junctions forward, VCE ≈ 0.2 V, IC = IC(sat) < β·IB, ON switch. Reverse active: roles swapped, very low gain, rarely used.

Key points

  • Cutoff: both junctions reverse, IC ≈ 0, VCE ≈ VCC: OFF switch.
  • Active: EBJ forward, CBJ reverse, IC = β·IB: amplification.
  • Saturation: both forward, VCE ≈ 0.2 V, IC < β·IB: ON switch.
  • Reverse active: roles swapped, very low gain, rarely used.
